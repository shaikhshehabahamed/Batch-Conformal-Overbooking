# Batch-Level Conformal Risk Control for Overbooking Decisions

An overbooking decision needs a valid upper bound on the total arrivals in a batch of bookings, not on any single booking. Bookings in the same batch often share correlated causes, so treating them as independent can be misleading. Standard parametric methods assume independence and can under-cover when real correlation is present. We propose a split-conformal procedure that treats historical batches, not individual bookings, as the exchangeable calibration unit. This gives a finite-sample-valid upper bound on total batch attendance under any within-batch dependence. In a simulated correlation sweep, our method is the only one of five compared methods to hold 90% nominal coverage at every correlation level. It is significantly narrower than every other valid method, and its margin over a calibrated distributionally robust baseline grows from 15% to 36% as correlation strengthens. On two real datasets, medical appointments and hotel bookings, a random split confirms this result. A realistic time-ordered split then exposes a genuine coverage failure, 70.9% against a 90% target, caused by measured drift in the attendance rate between calibration and test periods. A first-order formula, using only calibration-period data, predicts this failure to within 0.83 percentage points. Adaptive Conformal Inference restores coverage to 92.1% and remains the tightest valid method throughout. Translating the bound into a booking policy shows interval-level tightness does not always lower realized cost, a limitation we quantify directly. This gives overbooking decisions a distribution-free guarantee that existing conformal risk-control methods do not provide at the batch level.
